## 4. Institutional Policy & Power Sector Benchmark Documents

12. **World Bank (2020)**
    * **Title:** *Technical Note 2: Igniting Economic Growth by Reforming Nigeria's Power Sector*
    * **Project / Identifier:** World Bank Project P176224 / Document No. P1762240038c17010bfb5087a5bcc325b5
    * **Direct Source Link:** [World Bank Document Repository](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099061723133022449/pdf/P1762240038c17010bfb5087a5bcc325b5.pdf)
    * **Direct Local File:** `Literature/World Bank Document.pdf`
    * **Key Ground-Truth Data Points for Paper:**
      * **Macroeconomic Loss:** Annual economic losses from lack of reliable power in Nigeria stand at **US$25 billion** (5% to 7% of national GDP).
      * **Transmission Deficit & Grid Losses:** 85 million Nigerians (43%) lack access; DISCOs experience Aggregate Technical, Commercial, and Collection (ATC&C) losses near **50%** (vs. 15% international standard).
      * **Captive Genset Fleet:** Over **22 million captive diesel/gasoline generators** operate in Nigeria; their aggregate capacity is **8 times greater** than the operational capacity of the entire national transmission grid.
      * **Commercial & Industrial Real Power Costs:** Due to frequent grid failures, small commercial enterprises spend **US$0.40/kWh** and large industrial enterprises spend **US$0.46/kWh** on captive power—nearly 3× higher than cost-reflective utility tariffs ($0.14/kWh).
      * **Strategic Justification:** Provides irrefutable institutional validation for why Nigerian AI data centers must invest in high-penetration solar-storage microgrids rather than relying on grid imports or captive diesel.
